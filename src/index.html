01 About

I'm the person you put on the requirement nobody can pin down.

Most of my work starts where the spec runs out — the requirement everyone agrees matters and no one can quite describe.

For ten years I built enterprise systems — Java platforms, data integrations, the unglamorous plumbing that has to be right. For the last five I led the teams that build them. That decade isn't the headline anymore, but it's the foundation underneath everything I do now: I know the shape of what needs building, so I can tell when AI is right — and when it's confidently wrong.

What I care about is judgment: choosing the technology a problem actually needs, not the one that's familiar. That's also why AI adoption is the work I lead now. Used well, it changes how a team designs, reviews, and decides. Used carelessly, it just adds noise — I help teams tell the difference.

02 Approach

AI doesn't replace your experts — it magnifies them. Adoption is teaching a team to steer it, not fear it.

Magnify, don't replace. AI is a multiplier for people who know their domain — the expert, not the tool, judges if the output is right.

Meet people where they are. Keep coding by hand if you like — we still use AI for the docs, the gaps we overlooked, the brainstorm before the build.

Experience is the steering wheel. Small tasks, clear intent, challenge its reasoning — that's what keeps AI from hallucinating into your codebase.

And it's not all magic. AI is only as good as the context it's given — point it at a messy, undocumented codebase and it struggles like anyone would. Knowing that limit is part of using it well.
